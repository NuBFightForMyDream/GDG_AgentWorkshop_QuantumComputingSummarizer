Summary
• Representation in Different Bases: The core concept emphasized is representing
quantum states in various bases, analogous to expressing the exact same meaning
in different languages (e.g., English vs. Cantonese).
• Role of Unitary Matrices: Unitary matrices are used to transform vectors from
one representation to another by rotating the basis in hyperspace.
• The Translation Analogy: In this context, the matrix acts as a "dictionary," and
the rotation represents the actual transformation or translation process between the
two "languages" (bases).
• Mathematical Foundations: The text notes the importance of the concept of
completeness and the ability to construct matrices using eigenvalues and
eigenvectors.
• Relevance to Quantum Computing: These mathematical skills are fundamental
tools that will be essential for future quantum computing applications.

Multi-Qubits
• Qubit stands for quantum bit. It refers to a vector space with two basis vectors and it is 2D. We are very familiar
with this already. The vector in this vector space can be represented by a qubit, which is the linear combination
of the basis vectors. And the basis vectors can be ȁ ⟩0 / ȁ ⟩1 and ȁ ⟩
+ / ȁ ⟩
− in a single spin system.
• We just discussed that we can form a larger system with two qubits and the number of basis vectors becomes
four. Since the tensor product is used to construct a larger system from its subsystems through the permutation
of basis vectors, the number of basis states grows exponentially and this is the power of quantum computing.
• For a single qubit, we call it a ℂ2 space, which means it is a 2D complex vector space ( ℂ means complex).
When there are 2 qubits, the space is ℂ4 as it is a 4-dimensional due to the tensor product of two ℂ2 spaces. E.g.
for n qubits, it is a tensor product of two 𝑛ℂ2 spaces and the dimension is 2𝑛 instead of n.
So, if there are 10 qubits, the dimension is now 1024 and there are 1024 basis states! A vector (state) in the 10-
qubit system will be a superposition of 1024 basis states which will enable quantum parallelism.
• Since it is just the permutation of the basis vectors of single-qubits, then it is just the permutation of 0’s and 1’s.
Therefore, they are just the binary numbers from 0 to 2𝑛
− 1 for n-qubits. This helps us to memorize the basis
vectors and also allows us to write them succinctly in decimal numbers in the kets.

Bit (MSB) and which is the Least Significant Bit (LSB). For the spins of two electrons, A and B, we may write
