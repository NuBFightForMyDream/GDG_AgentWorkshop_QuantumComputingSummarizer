Unitary Matrix
• In quantum computing and quantum mechanics, the unitary matrix is very important.
This is as important as the Hermitian matrix.
• A matrix is an operator that rotates the vector (state). A Hermitian matrix is a matrix that is equal to its adjoint
matrix, i.e. 𝐴† = 𝐴, where 𝐴† is the adjoint matrix of A by performing the complex conjugate and transpose
operations.
• The Hermitian matrix is important because it has real eigenvalues and therefore, all observables must
correspond to Hermitian matrices.
• The unitary matrix is important because it preserves the inner product of vectors when they are transformed
together by a unitary matrix. It also preserves the length of a vector. This is very important because it will
preserve the probability amplitude of a vector in quantum computing so that it is always 1.

Summary
• Finding Eigenvalues: A new, often more convenient, method for finding the eigenvalues of a matrix was
derived.
• Diagonalization: If a matrix is represented using its own eigenvectors as the basis, it becomes a diagonal
matrix where the diagonal elements are its eigenvalues.
• Unitary Matrix: The concept of a unitary matrix was introduced, which is crucial in quantum
computing.
• Properties of Unitary Matrices:
• They preserve the inner product between any two vectors after transformation.
• They preserve the magnitude (norm) of a vector.
• Their rows and columns form an orthonormal set.
• Unitary Test: A key method to determine if a matrix U is unitary is to check if it transforms an
orthonormal set of basis vectors into another orthonormal set. This principle will be used later to verify
quantum oracles.
